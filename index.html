02 Abstract

Low-latency diffusion control through cross-cycle refinement.

Diffusion policies model multimodal action distributions effectively, but iterative denoising can introduce substantial latency in closed-loop robotic manipulation. PASDP distributes action generation and updating across successive control cycles. During training, it applies stronger Gaussian corruption to distal action positions, near-clean inputs to proximal positions, and greater objective weight to actions closer to execution. During inference, a sliding action buffer removes the executed action, appends Gaussian noise at the distal end, and re-predicts the complete buffer from the latest observation.

Each policy inference uses one network evaluation, while a future execution slot can be updated over multiple cycles before reaching the buffer head. Across 10 Robomimic tasks, PASDP reduced policy-inference latency by more than one order of magnitude while maintaining competitive average success. On two real-world conveyor-manipulation tasks, it achieved 11 ms inference latency and improved task-completion outcomes over Diffusion Policy under the evaluated conditions.

03 Method

System overview

Position organizes both training and control.

A buffer position encodes how long remains before execution: distal actions start under stronger corruption and are repeatedly updated as they move toward the head.

  1. TRAIN / INPUT

    Position-dependent corruption

    Distal positions receive stronger Gaussian corruption; the final position receives pure noise.

  2. TRAIN / LOSS

    Position-weighted objective

    Actions closer to execution receive greater loss weight because they immediately determine robot motion.

  3. CONTROL / LOOP

    Streaming refinement

    The buffer shifts after execution, receives noise at its tail, and is re-predicted once from the latest observation.
