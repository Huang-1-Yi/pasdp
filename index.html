Diffusion policies model multimodal action distributions effectively, but their iterative denoising process can introduce substantial latency in closed-loop robotic manipulation. Continuous control offers a different allocation of this computation: the next action must be available immediately, whereas later actions can be updated after new observations arrive. We therefore propose the Position-Aware Streaming Diffusion Policy (PASDP), which distributes action generation and updating across successive control cycles. During training, PASDP applies stronger Gaussian corruption to distal action positions and near-clean inputs to proximal positions. A position-weighted objective assigns greater importance to actions closer to execution. During inference, PASDP maintains a sliding action buffer. Each control cycle removes the executed action, appends Gaussian noise at the distal end, and re-predicts the complete buffer from the latest observation. One network evaluation thus generates a new distal action and updates the unexecuted predictions carried from the previous cycle. Each policy inference uses one network evaluation, while a future execution slot can be updated over multiple cycles before reaching the buffer head. On 10 Robomimic tasks, PASDP reduced policy-inference latency by more than one order of magnitude while maintaining competitive average success. On two real-world conveyor-manipulation tasks, it achieved an inference latency of 11 ms and improved task-completion outcomes over Diffusion Policy under the evaluated conditions.

Position-Aware Streaming Diffusion Policy

PASDP uses the action position to organize both training and closed-loop inference. During training, the corruption level decreases from the buffer tail to the execution head. During inference, the same ordering is used in a persistent action buffer. A future execution slot enters at the tail and is re-predicted after each buffer shift until it reaches the head.

Position-Dependent Corruption

In the standard N = 1 mode, the position index specifies both the order within the prediction horizon and the number of buffer shifts remaining before execution. Distal positions receive stronger Gaussian corruption, while the final position receives pure Gaussian noise.

Position-Weighted Objective

Proximal actions directly determine the next robot motion. Distal predictions remain in the buffer and can be updated before execution. PASDP therefore assigns greater loss weight to positions near the execution head.

Sliding-Buffer Streaming Inference

After executing the head action, PASDP shifts the remaining predictions toward the buffer head and appends Gaussian noise at the tail. The network then re-predicts the complete buffer once from the latest observation. One policy inference contains one network evaluation.

Experiments

Simulation Experiments

Experiments are conducted on 10 Robomimic tasks. Under the reported common prediction horizon of H = 40, PASDP achieves an average success rate of 77.0% with an inference latency of approximately 18 ms. Diffusion Policy achieves an average success rate of 75.9% with an inference latency of 261 ms.
